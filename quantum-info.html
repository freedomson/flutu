What this experience does—and does not do

Quantum Fluctuations and the Many-Worlds Idea

This decision page turns an external random result into a simple suggestion: right or left. The physics behind quantum random numbers is fascinating, but this site's suggestion is a playful experience—not a measurement of fate or proof that a person splits across universes.

Where does the choice come from?

When you click “Choose for me,” the server requests a quantum random permutation from the Australian National University (ANU) generator. It receives a permutation of the values 1 and 2, uses the last value to select one of the two directions, and displays only that direction. The set is not shown in the interface.

The random result is only used to select the app's suggestion. It does not make your decision for you: you remain free to follow the suggestion, choose the other way, or do neither.

What is a “quantum fluctuation”?

In physics, the term usually describes quantum variations in physical quantities, such as fields, which play a role in theories and experimentally studied phenomena. A random bit produced by a quantum device may depend on a measurement of a quantum system, but this app does not directly measure a fluctuation in your body, mind, or surroundings. It only queries ANU's external service.

What about the many-worlds interpretation?

The many-worlds interpretation is one interpretation of quantum mechanics. In simplified terms, it describes quantum evolution as branching states that stop interfering with one another through decoherence. It is a theoretical interpretation; there is no consensus that it is the correct description of reality, and its branches are not worlds we can visit or send messages between.

People sometimes use the analogy, “in one branch I chose right; in another, left.” This image may help illustrate the interpretation, but it does not show that your consciousness or presence is copied, travels, or splits. This page cannot identify, create, or observe versions of you in parallel universes.

In short: quantum randomness inspires the app's choice; the connection to “taking different paths in different universes” is a hypothetical reflection based on one interpretation of quantum mechanics, not a proven effect of this experience.
